Why It Matters

Troubleshooting

Common Microphone Problems This Test Reveals

If a check fails above, here's what it usually means.

Microphone Access Denied

Usually a browser permission was blocked. Check your site settings and allow microphone access, then reload this mic test page.

No Signal Detected

The meter staying flat usually means the wrong input device is selected, the mic is muted at the OS level, or a cable is loose.

Level Too Quiet

A consistently low meter reading often means input gain is turned down in your system sound settings, or you're too far from the mic.

Constant Clipping

If the clipping badge keeps triggering, your input gain is too high — lower it in your OS audio settings to avoid distorted recordings.

High Background Noise

A high noise floor while silent can point to electrical interference, a faulty cable, or a fan or AC unit too close to the microphone.

Distorted or Muffled Playback

If your recorded clip sounds muffled or crackly on playback, check for a damaged mic capsule, a covered port, or a corrupted driver.

The level meter shows your input in real time, including a clipping warning if your voice is overdriving the input. Low levels often mean the wrong device is selected or input gain is turned down in your OS sound settings; constant clipping means the gain is too high.

The spectrum analyzer breaks your voice into frequency bands from low bass to high treble. A healthy microphone shows energy moving naturally across bands as you speak; a flat or missing range can indicate a faulty capsule or a software filter cutting frequencies.
