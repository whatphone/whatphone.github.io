How to use the cell signal strength checker

  1. Find your readings. Open your phone's field test or SIM status screen and note RSRP and SINR. Add RSRQ or RSSI if you have them.
  2. Enter the values. Type them in with their minus signs. RSRP is usually between −44 and −140 dBm.
  3. Read the rating. The overall rating uses your weakest metric, and the table explains each number.
  4. Convert or plan a booster. Use the ASU converter for Android values, or enter the outside signal and booster gain to estimate the indoor result.

What is a good cell signal strength?

Signal bars are a rough guide and differ between brands. The numbers behind them are far more useful. On 4G and 5G, RSRP is the main strength reading. A value of −80 dBm or higher is excellent, −80 to −90 is good, −90 to −100 is fair and below −100 is weak. These are common industry guidelines, and carriers and equipment makers draw the lines in slightly different places.

Strength is only half the story

SINR measures how clean the signal is. Strong RSRP with low SINR means interference or a busy cell, which is why you can have full bars and still get slow data. For the best speeds look for good RSRP together with SINR above 13 dB. Read our cell signal strength guide for ways to improve a weak signal.

Frequently asked questions

For 4G and 5G RSRP, −80 dBm or higher is excellent, −80 to −90 good, −90 to −100 fair and below −100 weak. The closer to zero, the stronger the signal. Thresholds vary slightly between sources.

RSRP is the power of the cell's reference signal, RSRQ is signal quality relative to total power, SINR is the signal-to-noise ratio and RSSI is total received power including noise. RSRP and SINR are the most useful for judging real-world performance.

On many iPhones you can dial *3001#12345#* to open Field Test mode, though the layout depends on the model and iOS version. On Android open Settings → About phone → SIM status, or dial *#0011# on some Samsung phones.

Bars mostly reflect signal strength. Slow data can come from poor SINR, a congested cell or a weak connection to the wider network, so check SINR and RSRQ too.

ASU is the Android Signal Unit, a simple integer scale. On LTE the signal in dBm equals ASU minus 140. On 3G it is ASU minus 116, and on 2G GSM it is two times ASU minus 113.

A booster amplifies the signal you already receive, so it needs some usable outside signal. Gain is reduced by cable loss, and it cannot help if there is no signal outside.
