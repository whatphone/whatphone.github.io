Why It Matters

More Than a Speed Number

Most WiFi speed tests stop at download and upload. This one measures the two metrics that actually explain a choppy Zoom call or a laggy match: jitter and packet loss.

Real Jitter Measurement

We fire a rapid burst of pings and measure how much the delay between them varies — the exact instability that causes stuttering audio and frozen video frames.

Packet Loss Detection

Every dropped or timed-out request during the ping burst is logged, giving you an estimated packet loss percentage most consumer speed tests never show.

Accurate Ping

Ping is averaged across many samples rather than a single request, so one lucky or unlucky round trip can't skew your result.

Live Download & Upload

Speeds update in real time as data streams to and from a nearby Cloudflare edge server, so you can watch throughput rise and dip as your connection actually behaves.

Works on Any Device

Runs the same way in a phone browser on WiFi or cellular, on a laptop, or on a smart TV browser — no app store, no install, no sign-up.

100% Private

The test exchanges throwaway data with the network — nothing about your device, files, or browsing is read, stored, or uploaded anywhere.

The Process

How the Test Runs

Four short phases, roughly 15 seconds total, run automatically once you hit start.

Ping Burst

A rapid series of small requests is sent to measure round-trip time, giving us ping, jitter, and any dropped requests.

Download Test

Data streams down from a nearby edge server for a few seconds while we track how fast the bytes actually arrive.

Upload Test

Your browser sends data back out, measuring the return path — often the weaker direction on home WiFi.

Full Report

All five results appear together with a plain-language verdict on whether your connection can handle calls, streaming, or gaming.

Troubleshooting

What a Bad Result Usually Means

If a number here comes back worse than expected, here's where to look first.

High Packet Loss

Usually router interference or distance. Move closer to the router, switch to the 5GHz band, or check for other devices flooding the same channel.

High Jitter

Often caused by a congested network — someone else streaming or downloading, or a VPN adding an inconsistent extra hop. Pause other traffic and re-test.

Slow Download, Fine Upload

Can point to ISP throttling or evening congestion in your neighborhood. Re-test at a different time of day to see if it's load-related.

Test Stalls or Fails

An ad blocker, VPN, or firewall may be blocking the test's requests. Try disabling extensions for this page or testing in a private window.

FAQ

WiFi Speed Test — Common Questions

Everything you need to know about reading your download, upload, jitter, and packet loss results.

Most video call platforms only need 3-4 Mbps of download and upload speed for HD quality. If your download and upload numbers here are comfortably above that but calls still freeze, the real culprit is usually jitter or packet loss, not raw speed.

Jitter is the variation in delay between packets arriving at your device. Video and audio calls stream in a steady rhythm, so when packets arrive at uneven intervals, the app's buffer runs dry and you hear robotic audio or see frozen frames, even if your average speed looks fine.

Under 1% packet loss is generally unnoticeable. 1-2.5% starts to cause occasional glitches in calls and streams. Above 2.5% is considered high and will cause regular freezing, dropped calls, and rubber-banding in online games.

WiFi speed and your internet plan speed are two different things. Distance from the router, wall interference, band congestion (2.4GHz vs 5GHz), and the number of connected devices all reduce the WiFi speed you actually get, even when your ISP is delivering the full plan speed to your router.
