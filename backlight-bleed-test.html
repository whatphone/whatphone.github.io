How to use the backlight bleed test

  1. Prepare the room. Turn the lights down and raise screen brightness to maximum. Disable auto-brightness and night mode.
  2. Start with Black. Look at the edges and corners. Glowing light on a black screen is the classic backlight bleed sign.
  3. Check Gray and White. Gray at a low level exposes clouding and tints. White shows uneven brightness from one side to the other.
  4. Inspect the Gradient and Steps. Smooth gradients should show no hard stripes. Visible bands mean color banding.
  5. Tick what you see. Use the checklist for an overall verdict and copy the report to share with a seller or support team.

What is backlight bleed?

Backlight bleed is light that leaks around the edges of an LCD panel, usually because of uneven pressure between the frame and the panel layers. It is most obvious on black backgrounds in a dark room. OLED screens have no backlight, so they cannot show bleed. They can still show uneven tints on dark gray, which this test also reveals.

Bleed vs. IPS glow vs. clouding

Backlight bleed stays in the same place when you move your head. IPS glow is a soft sheen in the corners that changes with viewing angle. Clouding looks like faint lighter blotches across the panel, often visible on dark gray. A small amount of all three is common even on good displays.

When to return a device

Judge by what you see in normal use, not by a camera photo in a pitch-black room. If bright patches are visible on dark content in a lit room, or one side is clearly tinted, consider an exchange within your return window. For other display checks, try the dead pixel test and the OLED burn-in test.

Color banding

Banding appears when a display cannot show enough shades, so a smooth gradient turns into visible steps. The Gradient and Steps patterns make this easy to spot. Mild banding at low brightness is common on 8-bit panels.
