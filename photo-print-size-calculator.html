How to use the photo print size calculator

  1. Enter your photo's pixels. Type the width and height from the photo's details, or tap a preset such as 12 MP or 48 MP.
  2. Pick a target quality. 300 PPI suits prints held in the hand. 200 PPI is fine for a framed wall print.
  3. Read the largest print size. The result shows the biggest print that keeps your target sharpness, plus the biggest standard paper size that fits.
  4. Check any size. Enter a custom width and height to see its PPI, or scan the table for common sizes and A-series paper.

How big can you print a phone photo?

Print size depends on how many pixels your photo has and how densely you want them packed. Divide the pixel width by the PPI to get inches. A 12 megapixel photo of 4032 × 3024 pixels prints at about 13.4 × 10.1 inches at 300 PPI, and larger at a lower PPI if the print is viewed from further away.

What PPI do you need?

300 PPI is the usual target for prints viewed up close. Wall pictures viewed from a distance look fine at 150 to 200 PPI, because your eye cannot resolve the pixels from far away. The quality labels here are practical guidelines, not strict rules.

Why megapixels are not everything

A sharp 12 MP photo can print better than a blurry 50 MP one. Light, focus and motion matter as much as pixel count. Read the phone photo print size guide for tips on exporting and ordering prints.
