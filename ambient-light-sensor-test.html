Features

What This Light Sensor Test Does

A simple, interactive check for your phone's ambient light sensor and auto-brightness.

Live Lux Readings

Reads the AmbientLightSensor API where available to display the current illuminance in lux, updated in real time as the light around you changes.

Cover / Uncover Test

Cover the top of your phone with your hand — the lux reading should drop sharply. Remove it and it should rise again, confirming the sensor responds.

Lux History Graph

A rolling 120-sample graph plots your lux readings over time, so you can see how the sensor responds to changes in lighting instantly.

Auto-Brightness Preview

A simulated screen dims and brightens in response to the lux reading, illustrating how auto-brightness would adjust your display in real use.

Environment Classification

Automatically classifies the current reading — dark room, indoor, overcast, bright day — so you can quickly interpret your lux value.

100% Private

Everything runs locally in your browser. No data is transmitted, stored, or shared — just a simple, private light sensor check.

FAQ

Ambient Light Sensor Test — Common Questions

Everything you need to know about testing your phone's light sensor.

Open this page on your phone, tap "Start Light Test", and allow sensor access. The live lux reading shows the current light level. Cover the top of your phone with your hand — the lux value should drop sharply. Remove your hand and it should rise again. This confirms whether your ambient light sensor is working.

Typical lux values vary by environment: a dim room is around 10–50 lux, a normal indoor room is 100–300 lux, an office or bright indoor space is 300–500 lux, an overcast day outdoors is 1,000–10,000 lux, and direct sunlight can exceed 100,000 lux. Covering the sensor with your hand should bring the reading close to 0 lux.

The AmbientLightSensor API is currently supported only on Chrome for Android and a few other Chromium-based mobile browsers. Safari on iOS and most desktop browsers do not expose ambient light sensor data to web apps. If your browser doesn't support it, the test will show as unsupported — but the auto-brightness behavior can still be observed with the visual meter.

Common causes include a dirty or obstructed ambient light sensor (often near the earpiece), a screen protector covering the sensor, a software glitch, or auto-brightness being disabled in settings. Try cleaning the top of the screen, removing any case or protector near the sensor, toggling auto-brightness off and on in settings, and restarting the device. If the lux reading here doesn't change when you cover the sensor, the hardware may need repair.
