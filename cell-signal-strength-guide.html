What Is a Good Cell Signal Strength in dBm?

Good and bad dBm values, how to read RSRP and SINR, where to find them on your phone and what to do about a weak signal.

October 29, 2026
5 minute read
WhatPhone Editorial

Four bars should mean a good connection, yet sometimes you have full bars and a page will not load. Bars are only a rough picture. The real numbers, measured in dBm and dB, show how strong and how clean your signal actually is, and they tell you what to fix.

Have your numbers already? Type them into our cell signal strength checker for an instant rating.

Step 1: Learn the Four Numbers

Four readings appear in field test screens. They are all negative dBm or dB values, and closer to zero is better for the power readings.

MetricExcellentGoodFairPoor
RSRP (strength)−80 dBm or higher−80 to −90−90 to −100Below −100
RSRQ (quality)−10 dB or higher−10 to −15−15 to −20Below −20
SINR (clean signal)20 dB or higher13 to 200 to 13Below 0
RSSI (total power)−65 dBm or higher−65 to −75−75 to −85Below −85
💡
Note: These thresholds follow commonly published industry guides. Carriers and equipment makers draw the lines in slightly different places, so treat them as guidelines.

RSRP is the main strength reading on 4G and 5G, and SINR is the best guide to speed because it shows how much interference you are fighting. RSRQ reflects quality and congestion, and RSSI includes everything the phone hears, noise too.

Step 2: Find the Numbers on Your Phone

1

iPhone

On many iPhones, open the Phone app, dial *3001#12345#* and tap call to open Field Test mode, then look for the serving cell measurements. The layout depends on the model and iOS version, and it may not be available on every model.

2

Android

Open Settings → About phone → SIM status and look for Signal strength. Some brands show RSRP, RSRQ and SINR there, and on some Samsung phones you can dial *#0011# for a service mode screen.

3

Use an app if your phone hides the data

Network info apps for Android can display RSRP and SINR in real time. Menus differ by phone, carrier and software version.

Step 3: Why Bars Can Mislead You

Bars mostly reflect strength, and each brand draws them differently. They say little about interference or congestion. A strong RSRP with a poor SINR can still give slow data, which is common near busy towers, on crowded networks and in dense city blocks. If speeds are slow, check SINR first. You can then test the actual speed with our speed test.

Step 4: Improve a Weak Signal

1

Move to a better spot

Go near a window, outdoors or to a higher floor. Walls, metal roofs and low-emissivity glass block signal, and a few steps can change your RSRP by 10 dB or more.

2

Turn on Wi-Fi calling

If your carrier supports it, Wi-Fi calling routes calls over your Wi-Fi network when the cell signal is poor.

3

Toggle airplane mode or restart

This forces the phone to reconnect and sometimes switch to a better tower or band.

4

Try LTE instead of 5G

If 5G is weak at your location, setting your phone to LTE can give a steadier connection. See our 5G optimization guide.

5

Update carrier settings and software

Updates can improve band support and signal handling.

6

Check your SIM and carrier

A different carrier or a second eSIM may have a stronger tower where you live. Our eSIM setup guide shows how to add one.

✅
Pro tip: Test at the same spot at different times of day. If SINR drops in the evening while RSRP stays steady, the tower is probably congested rather than far away.

Step 5: Know When a Signal Booster Helps

A booster amplifies the outside signal and re-broadcasts it indoors. The result is roughly the outside signal plus the booster's gain, minus cable and splitter losses. For example, −110 dBm outside plus 50 dB of gain minus 3 dB of loss gives about −63 dBm inside. A booster cannot create a signal where there is none, and its real gain is limited by its output power. Use a booster approved in your country, and compare it with a carrier network extender or Wi-Fi calling first.
