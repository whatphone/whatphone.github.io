How to Perform a Vibration Test on Your Phone

Check whether your vibration motor works, how strong it is and whether a weak buzz is a setting or a hardware fault, on Android and iPhone.

Oct 28, 2026
8 minute read
WhatPhone Editorial

A weak buzz on an incoming call, a keyboard that no longer "clicks" or notifications you keep missing can all point to a failing vibration motor. A phone vibration test takes about a minute and tells you whether the motor works, how strong it is and whether the problem is hardware or just a setting.

The fastest check is the free WhatPhone vibration test, which fires short, long and pulsing patterns from your browser on Android. The rest of this guide covers the built-in options on Android and iPhone, useful apps and how to read what you feel.

What a vibration test checks

A vibration test is mostly a feel test. You trigger the motor and judge what happens. A good test checks:

  • Whether it works at all — does the phone buzz when told to?
  • Strength — is the buzz as firm as it used to be, at low, medium and high settings?
  • Patterns — can the motor do short taps, long buzzes and repeating pulses cleanly, or does it lag or fade?
  • Consistency — does it work every time, or only sometimes?
  • Noise — a smooth hum is normal; rattling or grinding is not.
💡
Two motor types: older and budget phones use an ERM motor (a small off-centre weight that spins and gives a long, loose buzz). Most modern flagships, including the iPhone's Taptic Engine, use a linear actuator that produces crisp, short taps. Expect different feel depending on your model; check your model if you're unsure.

Built‑in vibration test on Android

Android doesn't have one universal test button, but every phone lets you trigger and adjust vibration in settings.

1

Open the vibration settings

On Google Pixel and most stock Android phones go to Settings → Sound & vibration → Vibration & haptics. On Samsung go to Settings → Sounds and vibration → Vibration intensity.

2

Turn each option on and feel it

Enable Vibrate for calls, Notification vibration and Touch feedback, then use the sliders to move from low to high. Each time you change a slider, the phone previews the vibration.

3

Preview a ringtone or alarm vibration

Switch the ringer to vibrate with the volume key and set a quick alarm for the next minute with vibration enabled. Alarms are a reliable way to trigger a long buzz.

4

Use the manufacturer's diagnostics

Samsung phones often include a Phone diagnostics section in the Samsung Members app with a vibration check. On many Samsung models, dialling *#0*# opens a hardware test menu with a vibration tile. Availability depends on model and carrier.

5

Run a browser test

Open the WhatPhone vibration test in Chrome or Samsung Internet and tap each pattern. You'll be asked to confirm what you felt, and nothing is installed or uploaded.

Built‑in vibration test on iOS

iPhone has no vibration test button either, and Safari doesn't allow websites to trigger vibration, so web-based tests won't buzz an iPhone. That's a browser limit, not a broken Taptic Engine. Use these built-in options instead.

1

Check that haptics are on

Go to Settings → Sounds & Haptics and make sure System Haptics is on. Then turn on Vibrate on Ring and Vibrate on Silent.

2

Preview every vibration pattern

Tap Ringtone → Vibration. Each pattern, such as Alert, Heartbeat or Rapid, plays when you tap it, so you can test short and long buzzes in seconds.

3

Create a custom pattern

On the same screen choose Create New Vibration and tap out a rhythm. It's a handy way to test fine control of the motor.

4

Feel Haptic Touch and the keyboard

Press and hold an app icon or turn on keyboard haptics in Settings → Sounds & Haptics → Keyboard Feedback. These use the same motor.

5

Check the master switch

Go to Settings → Accessibility → Touch → Vibration. If this is off, all vibration is disabled, including emergency alerts.

✅
Pro tip: test on a hard, flat surface with the case off. A soft sofa or a thick case can make a healthy motor feel weak.

Third‑party apps for vibration testing

If you want more than the built-in options, a few diagnostics apps include a vibration check alongside tests for the screen, speakers, sensors and battery. Examples include Phone Doctor Plus and TestM; app lineups and features change, so check the current listing in your app store. Manufacturer tools such as Samsung Members are a safer first stop on supported phones.

  • Check permissions — vibration needs no sensitive permission. Be wary of a test app that asks for contacts, location or your microphone.
  • Read recent reviews — avoid apps overloaded with full-screen ads or that push a paid "cleaner".
  • Prefer an all-in-one tool — one app covering several hardware tests saves installing many.
  • Uninstall when done — a diagnostics app is a one-time job.

Interpreting test results

Compare what you felt with the outcomes below to decide whether it's a setting, a software glitch or a hardware fault.

1

A strong, even buzz on every pattern

Your motor is healthy. If you still miss alerts, the problem is a setting: raise the intensity slider, check Do Not Disturb or Focus modes, and make sure notification vibration is enabled for that app.

2

A weak or dull buzz

Remove the case, test on a hard surface and raise the intensity. If it's still weak, restart the phone and install pending updates. A motor that is weak on every test and every setting may be wearing out.

3

Rattling, grinding or a very loud buzz

The motor or its mounting may be loose, often after a drop. This is a hardware issue and worth a repair quote.

4

Works sometimes, or only in certain apps

This usually points to software. Restart, update, then try Safe Mode on Android or reset all settings on iPhone (this keeps your data) to rule out a faulty app.

5

No vibration at all

First confirm the settings and master switches above. If the built-in diagnostics and a second test also fail, the motor or its connector has probably failed, so contact the manufacturer or an authorised repair shop.

⚠️
Water or drop damage? If your vibration stopped right after either, don't keep testing or charging it. Moisture can damage the board. Switch the phone off and get it checked.

If vibration turns out to be fine, keep going with the rest of your hardware: try the dead pixel test for the screen, the speaker test for audio or the touchscreen test for touch response.
