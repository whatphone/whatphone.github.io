Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 6 vs Apple A20 Pro: 2nm Phone Chips Explained

What the 2 nm jump means, what Qualcomm and Apple claim, which phones have the new chips and what to look for when you buy.

What Does 2 nm Actually Mean?

The nanometer number is now a marketing name for a generation of manufacturing, not a literal measurement of a transistor. In practice, a newer process packs more transistors into the same space and lets them switch using less power. That means a chip can be faster at the same energy or use less energy for the same speed. Both Qualcomm and Apple use TSMC's 2 nm process, so any real difference between the chips comes from design, not from who has the better factory.

Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 6 and Extreme Gen 6

Qualcomm launched two chips at its Snapdragon Summit on September 22, 2026. Both use Qualcomm's custom Oryon CPU with two Prime cores that reach 5.0 GHz, billed as the first phone chip to hit that speed, plus six Performance cores up to 4.0 GHz. Compared with last year's Gen 5, Qualcomm claims the standard chip is 10% faster on the CPU and 37% more power efficient.

The Extreme version is a separate design aimed at top-end phones. Qualcomm claims 13% faster CPU performance, 44% faster graphics and 35% faster AI processing than Gen 5. Its NPU has 50% more shared memory, which Qualcomm says lets it run mixture-of-experts AI models of more than 30 billion parameters on the device. It also adds 8K video at 60 frames per second, 4K slow motion at 240 fps and AI super resolution and frame generation for gaming that can save up to 40% power in supported games.

Which phones use them

The Xiaomi 18 Pro and Pro Max have been on sale in China since September 23. Motorola's Signature 27, announced on November 5, is the first Extreme-powered phone to launch globally. Honor, OnePlus, iQOO and RedMagic have confirmed flagships, and rumors point to Snapdragon in Samsung's Galaxy S27 Ultra and S27 Pro. Treat the Samsung chip report as unconfirmed.

Apple A20 Pro

Apple's A20 Pro powers the iPhone 18 Pro, Pro Max and iPhone Duo. It has a 6-core CPU, a 7-core GPU and a 32-core Neural Engine. Apple focuses on sustained performance, saying it is up to 40% faster than the A19 Pro, and credits a vapor chamber that is three times larger. Sustained performance matters because phones slow down when they get hot, so holding speed during long gaming or video sessions is a real benefit. Read more in our iPhone Duo and iPhone 18 Pro guide.

On-Device AI Is the New Battleground

Both companies stress AI. Running models on the phone keeps data private, works without a connection and avoids cloud delays, but it needs fast memory and a strong NPU. That is why Qualcomm highlights the Extreme's extra shared memory and Apple highlights its 32-core Neural Engine. Memory is also becoming expensive, which we cover in our guide to rising phone prices. Apple's COO also said that limited availability of advanced chip manufacturing constrained iPhone, iPad and Mac supply in the September quarter.

What This Means When You Buy

  • Look at sustained performance, not peak. A chip that holds speed for 20 minutes beats one that spikes for 20 seconds.
  • Efficiency helps battery life. Qualcomm's biggest claim for the standard chip is a 37% efficiency gain, and real results depend on the phone's battery and software.
  • Do not overpay for the Extreme chip unless you need it. The standard 8 Elite Gen 6 shares the same CPU cores and process, and the Extreme's extra benefits are mostly in gaming, AI and top-end video.
  • Test your own phone. You can check display smoothness with our refresh rate test and touch response with the touch sampling rate test.
