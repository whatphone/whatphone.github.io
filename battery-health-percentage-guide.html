How to Calculate Your Battery Health Percentage

The battery health formula, how to find your capacity numbers, what the result means and when a replacement makes sense.

October 29, 2026
5 minute read
WhatPhone Editorial

Your phone says 100% every time you charge it, but that number only tells you how full the battery is, not how healthy it is. Battery health is a separate figure: how much energy the battery can hold today compared with when it was new. This guide shows how to work it out, what the result means and when it is time for a replacement.

For a quick answer, use our battery health calculator with your design and current capacity.

Step 1: Use the Battery Health Formula

Battery health is the current maximum capacity divided by the design capacity, times 100. A phone designed for 5,000 mAh that now holds 4,300 mAh has 4,300 ÷ 5,000 × 100 = 86% health. The same formula works for watt-hours and for laptop batteries.

Step 2: Find the Two Numbers

1

Design capacity

This is the rated capacity of the battery when new. Find it on the manufacturer's spec sheet, for example 5,000 mAh.

2

Current maximum capacity on iPhone

Open Settings → Battery → Battery Health & Charging. The Maximum Capacity percentage is already your battery health, so you only need the calculator for other phones.

3

Current capacity on Android

Some phones and newer Android versions show battery health or capacity in Settings or a diagnostics app. Menu names differ by brand and model. If yours does not, a battery app can estimate capacity after several full charge sessions.

4

Use a long-term average

Capacity estimates vary a little from day to day, so check a few times and use the typical value.

Step 3: Interpret Your Result

HealthMeaningWhat to do
90% or moreExcellentNothing, keep your habits
80% to 89%Good, normal wearKeep using it
70% to 79%WornExpect shorter days and plan a replacement
Below 70%Heavily wornReplace the battery soon

Battery life scales with health. A phone that lasted 10 hours when new will last about 8.6 hours at 86% health, which is why the calculator also shows your real runtime.

Step 4: Understand Charge Cycles

A cycle is the use of 100% of the battery in total, not a single charge. Using 50% one day and recharging, then 50% the next day, adds up to one cycle. Makers rate batteries to keep a set share of their capacity after a number of cycles. Apple, for example, says iPhone batteries are designed to keep up to 80% of capacity after 500 complete cycles, or 1,000 cycles on newer models. Use the cycle field in the calculator to estimate where you stand and how many months you have until 80%. Treat the result as a rough guide, because wear depends on heat, charge habits and battery chemistry.

💡
Note: Cycle-based estimates are approximate. Your actual capacity, measured by the phone, is always more reliable.

Step 5: Slow Down the Wear

  • Keep it cool — heat is the biggest enemy of lithium-ion batteries, so avoid charging in direct sun or under a pillow.
  • Avoid extremes — staying between about 20% and 80% is gentler than constant 0% to 100% cycles.
  • Use optimized or limited charging — many phones can pause at 80% or learn your routine overnight.
  • Use certified chargers — see our fast charging guide for what to buy.

Our battery health guide covers these habits in more detail.

Step 6: Decide Whether to Replace the Battery

Replacement makes sense when health is below about 80% and the phone no longer lasts your day, when it shuts down unexpectedly or when performance is throttled. A fresh battery from the manufacturer or an authorized repair shop is often far cheaper than a new phone. If the battery is swollen, the screen is lifting or the phone gets very hot, stop using it and have it checked.
