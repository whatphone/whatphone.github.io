Why Phones Cost More in 2026: The AI Memory Shortage Explained

How AI demand for memory chips is raising phone prices and removing cheap models, and practical ways to spend less.

November 12, 2026
5 minute read
WhatPhone Editorial

If your next phone costs more than your last one, you are not imagining it. A global shortage of memory chips, driven by artificial intelligence, is raising what phones cost to build and what you pay for them. This article explains what is happening, how big the effect is and what you can do about it. Figures are current as of early November 2026.

Why Is Memory in Short Supply?

AI data centers need huge amounts of memory, especially high-bandwidth memory that sits next to AI processors. Three companies make most of the world's DRAM: Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron. Analysts say that in late 2025 they shifted most of their capacity toward AI customers, who pay more per chip. Phones use different memory chips, but they come from the same factories, so less is left for phones, laptops and other devices. The result is higher prices for the memory in your phone.

How It Is Changing Phones

  • Higher prices. Counterpoint estimates that prices of existing phones have risen about 15% worldwide this year, while newly launched models are about 25% more expensive than last year's.
  • Cheap phones are disappearing. Chinese makers account for roughly 60% of global shipments, and Counterpoint says they have sharply cut their entry-level projects because memory costs hit low-priced phones hardest.
  • Fewer shipments. Counterpoint's December 2025 forecast expected global shipments to fall 2.1% in 2026, and it said mid-range and high-end phones saw material costs rise 10% to 15%.
  • Possible spec cuts. When memory is expensive, a maker may keep the price and trim the RAM or storage, so check the configuration, not just the model name.

Even Apple Feels It

On its November 2 earnings call, Apple's CFO said gross margin would have dropped about one percentage point sequentially, mainly because of higher memory costs, and its COO said Apple had reluctantly raised prices on a number of products. CEO John Ternus described memory pricing as unprecedented. The iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max also cost $100 more than last year's Pro models, and the new iPhone Duo is supply constrained. When Will It End?

Nobody can say for certain. New memory factories take years to build, and AI demand is still growing, so most analysts expect prices to stay high for some time. It is safest to plan for current prices rather than wait for a drop.

How to Save Money on Your Next Phone

  1. Buy only the storage you need. Extra storage is now an expensive upgrade. Use our storage calculator to estimate how many photos, videos and apps you will keep, and read how to free up space on a full phone.
  2. Consider last year's model or a refurbished phone. Previous generations lose less value than new launches, and they are not subject to the newest price increases.
  3. Replace the battery instead of the phone. A worn battery is the most common reason people upgrade. Check yours with the battery health calculator, then read the battery health percentage guide.
  4. Check software support. Phones with long update promises stay useful longer. Pixel 8 and newer phones generally receive seven years of updates, and Motorola's new Signature 27 is advertised with seven years of OS upgrades.
  5. Look after what you have. Protect the battery and keep an eye on charging costs and habits, so the phone lasts as long as possible.
✅
Pro tip: Before buying, compare prices over a few weeks and use trade-in offers. If a deal looks too cheap for the specs, check that the RAM and storage match what you expect.
