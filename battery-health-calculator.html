How to use the battery health calculator

  1. Enter the design capacity. Use the original battery size in mAh from the spec sheet, for example 5,000.
  2. Enter the current capacity. Use the maximum capacity your phone or a battery app reports today.
  3. Add cycles and age (optional). These give a cycle-based estimate and show roughly when you will reach 80%.
  4. Read the verdict. See your health percentage, lost capacity, real battery life and whether replacement is worth considering.

How to calculate battery health

Battery health compares what your battery can hold today with what it could hold when new. Divide the current maximum capacity by the design capacity and multiply by 100. A 5,000 mAh battery that now holds 4,300 mAh is at 86% health. Lithium-ion batteries lose capacity slowly with every charge cycle, and heat speeds that up.

What counts as good battery health?

Above 80% is generally considered healthy. Many manufacturers rate batteries to keep about 80% capacity after a set number of cycles. Between 70% and 80% you may notice shorter days, and below 70% replacement is worth considering. Our battery health guide explains how to slow the wear.

Cycles are not the same as charges

One cycle equals using 100% of the battery in total, so two charges from 50% to 100% make one cycle.

Frequently asked questions

Divide the current maximum capacity by the design capacity and multiply by 100. For example 4,300 mAh ÷ 5,000 mAh × 100 is 86%.

80% or higher is generally considered good. Apple says iPhone batteries are designed to keep up to 80% of their capacity after 500 complete cycles, or 1,000 on newer models, and many other makers give similar ratings.

Consider it when health drops to about 80% and the phone no longer lasts through your day, and plan for it below 70%. Swelling, overheating or sudden shutdowns mean you should stop using the battery and get it checked.

On iPhone, Settings → Battery → Battery Health & Charging shows Maximum Capacity as a percentage. On Android, some brands show it in their diagnostics app, and battery apps can estimate it over several charges.

A cycle is the use of 100% of the battery's capacity in total, not a single charge. Using 50% one day and 50% the next counts as one cycle.

Batteries can ship with slightly more than their rated capacity, and measurements are estimates, so a new battery can read above 100%.
