How Much Does It Cost to Charge a Phone?

Convert mAh to kWh, add charger losses and see the real yearly cost in your own currency.

November 05, 2026
5 minute read
WhatPhone Editorial

Charging a phone every day feels like it must add up on your electricity bill. It does not. A smartphone battery holds so little energy that a full year of daily charging costs about the same as a coffee, and often much less. Here is how to work out the exact figure for your phone and your electricity price.

Want the number now? Use the phone charging cost calculator and enter your battery size and price per kWh.

Step 1: Find the Energy in Your Battery

Convert the battery from mAh to watt-hours: Wh = mAh × volts ÷ 1,000. Most current phone batteries run at about 3.85 V, so a 4,500 mAh battery holds 4,500 × 3.85 ÷ 1,000 = about 17.3 Wh.

Step 2: Add Charger Losses

Charging is not perfectly efficient. Some energy is lost as heat in the charger, cable and phone, so the wall supplies more than the battery receives. A typical efficiency is about 75%, with older chargers near 65% and quality fast chargers near 85%. At 75%, our 17.3 Wh battery draws 17.3 ÷ 0.75 = 23.1 Wh from the wall, or 0.0231 kWh. Wireless charging generally wastes more energy than a cable.

Step 3: Multiply by Your Electricity Price

Electricity is billed per kilowatt-hour (kWh), also called a unit. Find the price on your bill and multiply: 0.0231 kWh × 0.15 per kWh = about 0.0035 per full charge, a third of a cent. Charging 80% of the battery every day for a year uses about 6.7 kWh.

Step 4: Compare It With Other Things

One full phone charge uses about as much energy as a 10 W LED bulb running for two hours. A year of phone charging is a small share of what a household uses in a month. If you want to cut your bill, reducing heating, cooling or water heating makes far more difference than changing how you charge.

What about tablets, earbuds and power banks?

The same method works for any rechargeable device. A tablet with a battery of around 30 Wh draws about 40 Wh from the wall per full charge at 75% efficiency, which is 0.04 kWh and roughly twice a phone. Wireless earbuds hold well under 1 Wh and cost almost nothing to charge. A power bank adds one more conversion, so charging a phone from it uses somewhat more total energy than charging directly. To see what a power bank can deliver, use our power bank calculator.

Why the number is so small

Electricity bills are dominated by devices that make heat or move air: heaters, air conditioners, water heaters and ovens. A phone battery stores roughly the energy of a bright LED bulb running for under two hours, so even charging every day barely registers. If your bill feels high, look at those big appliances first.

Step 5: Small Ways to Save

  • Use a quality charger — better efficiency means less wasted energy. See our fast charging guide.
  • Prefer a cable over wireless — wireless charging typically loses more energy as heat.
  • Unplug unused chargers — they draw very little, but it costs nothing to unplug.
  • Keep your battery healthy — a worn battery needs more frequent charging. Check yours with the battery health calculator.
