Complete Guide to Identify Any Smartphone Model

Wondering what phone you have? Find your exact model on Android and iPhone, identify an unknown handset from its hardware, and confirm it with free online tools.

Oct 28, 2026
9 minute read
WhatPhone Editorial

If you've ever stared at your phone and wondered "what phone do I have?", you're not alone. Buying a case, ordering a charger, checking for updates or selling a handset all start with the same answer: the exact model.

The quick answer: on Android, open Settings → About phone and read Model name. On iPhone, open Settings → General → About and read Model Name. If that doesn't work, or your phone won't turn on, the sections below cover every other way to find out what kind of phone you have. You can also run the free WhatPhone detector and get your device report in a second.

Why knowing your phone model matters

Your model decides what works with your phone and what doesn't. Knowing it helps you:

  • Buy the right accessories — cases, screen protectors, chargers and wireless charging pads are made for specific models, and sizes often differ by only a few millimetres.
  • Check software support — each manufacturer publishes how long a model receives Android or iOS updates and security patches.
  • Get the right repair parts and support — a screen or battery for one model rarely fits another, and support teams will ask for your model first.
  • Sell or trade in with confidence — buyers and trade-in programs price by exact model and storage size.
  • Look up specs and features — whether your phone supports eSIM, 5G bands, fast charging or satellite features depends on the model.
💡
Model name vs. model number: the model name is the marketing name (like "Galaxy S24" or "iPhone 15 Pro"). The model number is a code (like "SM-S921B" or "A2848") that identifies the exact hardware variant, including region and carrier version. Support pages and parts catalogues often need the number, not the name.

Using device settings on Android

Every Android phone lists its model in the same place, even though menu labels differ slightly by brand.

1

Open Settings and scroll to the bottom

Tap About phone (on some phones it's About device or System → About phone).

2

Read the model name and number

Look for Model name or Model. Many phones also show a separate Model number — Samsung usually starts with "SM-". On some brands the number is under Regulatory labels or Software information.

3

Check your Android version

The same screen shows Android version and often the security patch level, useful when you're checking update support.

4

Find your IMEI if you need it

Open the Phone app and dial *#06#. The IMEI identifies your individual handset (not just the model) and is what carriers and lookup services ask for.

✅
Pro tip: keep your IMEI and serial number private. Share the model name when asking for help online, never the IMEI.

Using device settings on iOS

iPhones make it easy too, and Apple shows both the friendly name and the technical model code.

1

Open Settings → General → About

The Model Name row shows the marketing name, for example "iPhone 15 Pro Max".

2

Tap Model Number to reveal the A-number

The first value looks like MTUS3LL/A. Tap it once and it switches to a shorter code that begins with A (for example A2849). Apple's support pages use this A-number to list every model.

3

Read the first letter of the long model number

M means a new retail unit, F a refurbished one, N a replacement unit and P a personalized (engraved) device. It's a quick way to check what you were really sold.

4

Look at the iOS version

The iOS Version row sits in the same list, and tells you whether your phone is on the latest software.

Can't open Settings? Sign in on Apple's support site or open the Apple Devices app on a Mac or PC; your connected iPhone is listed with its model. Your Apple Account page also lists every signed-in device with its model name.

Identifying hardware features (camera, ports)

If the screen is broken, the phone is locked or it simply won't power on, the body itself gives strong clues. Compare what you see to the photos on a phone-specs site such as GSMArena.

  • Camera layout — count the lenses and note the shape of the camera bump. A single vertical pill, a square island or a horizontal bar across the back each narrow things down quickly.
  • Charging port — iPhone 15 and newer use USB-C; iPhone 14 and older use Lightning. Most Android phones from the last several years use USB-C, while very old ones use micro-USB.
  • Front camera and notch — a notch, a punch-hole or a pill-shaped cutout (Apple's Dynamic Island appeared on iPhone 14 Pro and later) tells you roughly what generation you hold.
  • Buttons and slots — a headphone jack, microSD slot, S Pen slot, fingerprint reader or a physical SIM tray all help rule models in or out. Many recent phones sold in the US have no SIM tray at all.
  • Printed labels — some phones print the model number on the back or on the SIM tray, and the retail box label lists the model, IMEI and serial number.
💡
No power, no problem: your Google Account's "Your devices" page or your Apple Account's device list shows the model of every phone that has ever signed in, even if the screen is dead.

Online model lookup tools

When the settings screen isn't enough, free web tools can match what you know to a specific model.

1

Use the WhatPhone detector

Open WhatPhone in your phone's browser and tap Detect My Phone. It reads your browser's device data and shows brand, model, OS version, screen details and capabilities. Note: Apple hides the exact iPhone model from browsers, so for iPhones WhatPhone infers the most likely model from screen resolution — confirm it in Settings.

2

Search by model number

Type the model number (like "SM-S921B") into a search engine or the search box on GSMArena. Manufacturers' support pages also list a model number against each product.

3

Use the maker's identification page

Apple has an "Identify your iPhone model" support page that matches an A-number to a model, and you can enter a serial number on Apple's coverage page to see the model name. Samsung, Google, Xiaomi and others offer similar lookups on their support sites.

4

Try an IMEI lookup carefully

Public IMEI checkers can reveal brand and model. Use well-known services only, never share your IMEI in public comments, and don't pay for a basic model check.

Once you know your model, you can run quick hardware checks too: the vibration test, dead pixel test, touchscreen test and camera test all work straight from your browser.
