Detect and Fix Dead Pixels on Your Smartphone

Learn what dead and stuck pixels are, run a quick dead pixel test on your phone, try safe fixes and know when to claim a repair.

Oct 28, 2026
8 minute read
WhatPhone Editorial

A tiny black dot, a speck of green that never changes or a pinprick of colour on an otherwise clean screen are all signs of a pixel problem. A dead pixel test shows you exactly where the defects are in under two minutes, and what you do next depends on what you find.

The quickest way to check is the free WhatPhone dead pixel test, which fills your screen with solid colours so every defect stands out. This guide explains what you're looking for, how to run the test with and without apps, and when a repair or replacement is worth it.

Understanding dead pixels

Every pixel on your screen is made of red, green and blue sub-pixels. When something goes wrong with one of them, you get one of three defects:

  • Dead pixel — stays black on every colour because it receives no light. These are usually permanent.
  • Stuck pixel — stays lit in a single colour (red, green or blue) no matter what's on screen. These can sometimes be revived.
  • Bright (hot) pixel — stays white, most visible on dark backgrounds.

Causes include manufacturing defects, a hard drop or pressure on the screen, heat and ageing. Before you panic, rule out look-alikes: dust or a speck under a screen protector, smudges, and image retention or burn-in on OLED screens. Coloured lines, patches or flickering bands are a failing display, not a pixel issue.

💡
Tip: remove the screen protector and wipe the display with a microfibre cloth first. Many "dead pixels" turn out to be trapped dust that moves or disappears when cleaned.

Using built‑in pixel test tools

Neither iOS nor stock Android has a dedicated dead pixel button in Settings, but you still have good built-in options.

1

Use your maker's diagnostics if available

On many Samsung phones, dialling *#0*# opens a hardware test menu with Red, Green and Blue screen tiles, and the Samsung Members app offers phone diagnostics. Availability depends on model and carrier.

2

Use the WhatPhone dead pixel test

Open the dead pixel test in your browser, tap Start and cycle through black, white, red, green, blue and gray. It works on phones, tablets and monitors with no install.

3

Create your own with solid-colour images

Save pure black, white, red, green and blue images to your gallery and view each one full screen. It's slower but works on any phone.

4

On iPhone, ask Apple

iPhone has no on-device pixel test, so use the full-screen method above. If you find a defect, the Apple Support app or an Apple Store can run official diagnostics.

For the most reliable result:

  • Set brightness to maximum and switch off auto-brightness.
  • Disable True Tone, Night Light, Night Shift and dark mode tinting, which can mask defects.
  • Test in a dim room, hold the phone steady and look closely at every area, including the edges and corners.
  • On each colour, look for dots that don't match: black dots on bright colours, bright dots on black.
  • Note the position (for example upper left) and take a photo for later.

Recommended third‑party apps

Apps can add automatic colour cycling and a pixel-repair mode. Options include Phone Doctor Plus and TestM (both include display tests among other hardware checks), and the browser-based JScreenFix, which is built for LCD monitors. App lineups change often, so confirm the current listing and reviews before installing.

  • Free and light — a pixel test needs no special permissions, so decline any request for contacts, location or microphone.
  • Avoid miracle claims — no app can repair a truly dead (black) pixel; only a stuck coloured pixel can be revived.
  • Check compatibility with OLED — some repair modes are designed for LCD panels and don't help OLED phones.
⚠️
Flashing-light warning: pixel-repair tools flash rapidly changing colours. Don't use them if you or anyone nearby has photosensitive epilepsy, and keep the phone out of the hands of children.
1

Run the repair tool for 10 to 30 minutes

Place the flashing area over the stuck pixel. Check the screen after each cycle and stop if the pixel returns to normal.

2

Avoid pressing on the screen

Some guides suggest gently massaging the pixel. This risks cracking the glass or damaging the panel, and can void your warranty. Skip it on OLED and foldable screens.

3

Accept when it's permanent

If the pixel is still black or stuck after a couple of tries, it's likely a permanent hardware fault. That moves you to the next step.

When to seek repair

A single stuck pixel that you can only see on a solid colour is something many people live with. These situations deserve action:

  • You're within the return window — shops commonly allow returns or exchanges for a short period (often 14 to 30 days). Test your screen on day one.
  • The phone is under warranty — manufacturers have their own pixel policies, and many only replace a screen if defects exceed a set number or are clustered. Check your warranty terms and any care plan.
  • Defects are spreading or clustered — new pixels appearing over days, or several dead pixels close together, usually mean the panel is failing.
  • You see lines, bands, patches or flicker — these are display faults, not single pixels, and rarely fix themselves.
  • It started after a drop, pressure or water — get it inspected quickly before the damage spreads.
✅
Before any repair: back up your data, note your exact phone model, and get a quote from the manufacturer and one independent repairer. OLED screen replacements can cost a lot, so compare the price to the value of your phone.

Not sure whether your screen has other hidden problems? Try the OLED burn-in test, backlight bleed test and touchscreen test to get a full picture of your display's health.
