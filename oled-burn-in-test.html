What to Look For
Burn-in — permanent dark or discolored areas that stay visible on every pattern.
Image retention — a faint ghost image that fades over time with new content.
Uniformity — even color across the panel with no tint or brightness variation.

FAQ

OLED Burn-in Test — Common Questions

Everything you need to know about burn-in, retention, and testing your screen.

Open this page, tap "Start Burn-in Test", and cycle through the solid colors, gradients, and retention patterns. Look for areas that appear darker or retain a ghost image of the previous screen content — these are signs of burn-in or image retention. Use the timer to hold each test pattern for a few seconds and inspect it closely.

Image retention is a temporary ghost image that fades after some time showing different content. Burn-in is a permanent discoloration or dark spot that stays visible on the same areas of the screen. This test helps you spot both by displaying uniform colors and patterns that make any deviation obvious.

Solid gray, white, and mid-tone colors are best for spotting burn-in because they reveal subtle variations in pixel brightness. Pure red, green, and blue also help identify sub-pixel burn-in. Gradients reveal uniformity issues across the panel, and the retention patterns make ghost images stand out.

True burn-in is generally permanent and may require a panel replacement. Image retention is usually temporary and often fades on its own. Some OLED TVs and phones include a pixel refresh or panel refresh feature in their settings that can reduce the appearance of burn-in or retention.
