How to use the charging time calculator

  1. Enter battery capacity. Find the mAh rating in your phone's spec sheet or Settings. Most phones list 4,000–6,000 mAh.
  2. Enter charger power. Use the watt rating printed on your charger. If your phone supports less than the charger offers, add the phone's max input too.
  3. Set the charge range. Choose where you start and where you want to stop, for example 10% to 80%.
  4. Read the estimate. You get total time, a finish clock time and milestone times. Switch to Battery runtime to see how long a full charge lasts.

How phone charging time is calculated

The basic idea is simple: energy needed divided by charging power. Battery capacity in mAh is converted to watt-hours (Wh) by multiplying by the nominal voltage, so a 5,000 mAh battery at 3.85 V holds about 19.25 Wh. Divide by the power your phone really draws and you get an ideal charging time.

Why real charging takes longer

Three things stretch the ideal time. First, some energy is lost as heat, so efficiency is below 100%. Second, a phone only accepts as much power as its own charging circuit allows, no matter how powerful the charger. Third, lithium-ion batteries slow charging as they fill, with the biggest slowdown after about 80%. The typical taper option in this calculator models that slowdown.

mAh vs. Wh

mAh measures charge, not energy, so you cannot compare packs of different voltages by mAh alone. Wh is the fair comparison. It also matters for flights, where airline limits are normally stated in Wh.
