How to use the speaker cleaner

  1. Dry the outside and position the device. Wipe the surface, remove any case, set the media volume to about 80–100% and place the phone with the speaker grille facing down and uncovered.
  2. Choose water eject or dust sweep. Use the 165 Hz water mode for trapped water and the 200–1,500 Hz sweep for dust, lint and a generally muffled sound.
  3. Press Start cleaning. Keep the device away from your ears while it plays. You may see tiny droplets move at the grille. Stop at any time with the Stop button.
  4. Wipe, dry and repeat if needed. Wipe the grille with a soft cloth and repeat once or twice. Then run the speaker test to check left and right balance.

How the 165 Hz speaker cleaner sound works

A speaker cone moves air by vibrating. A low-frequency tone around 165 Hz, played in short pulses, makes the cone move with large, repeated strokes. This motion can shake water out of the small holes in the grille and break the surface tension that holds droplets in place. It is the same idea behind the water eject feature on smartwatches, and it is why a web tool can act as an online speaker fixer without any app.

Speaker dust remover: the frequency sweep

Dust and pocket lint are cleared differently. The sound frequency sweep slides from 200 Hz up to 1,500 Hz and back, so the membrane passes through many different resonances and shakes the grille mesh. This can loosen light surface dust, though it will not remove packed lint. A soft dry brush does the final job.

How to get water out of a phone speaker online

Dry the outside, place the speaker face down, and run the 165 Hz water eject cycle for 30 seconds. Wipe, then repeat once or twice. Let the phone air dry for several hours before charging. If you need a sound to blow water out of a phone speaker, this pulsed tone is the one most people use.

Fix a muffled phone speaker

Muffled, crackly or quiet audio is usually caused by moisture or debris at the grille, so cleaning with sound can help. If the problem remains after a few cycles, the speaker, the grille or the audio chip may be damaged, and a repair shop is the next step. You can also check for software issues with the full speaker test.

Fix speaker problems on your device

Fix iPhone speaker water and remove dust from the iPhone speaker grille

iPhones have no built-in water eject control, so a browser tool is a handy substitute. Use water mode with the bottom speaker facing down. For dust, gently brush the grille with a soft dry brush and never poke it with a pin.

Clean an Android phone speaker and eject water from a Samsung speaker

Run the cycle with the speaker facing down. Many Samsung phones warn about moisture in the charging port, so wait until the warning clears before you plug in. Phones from Xiaomi, Redmi, OnePlus and other brands often have a bottom speaker plus an earpiece that acts as a second speaker, so test both sides.

Fix iPad speaker sound that is muffled

iPads have speakers at the bottom or on the sides depending on model. Use the left and right tests to find which side is muffled, then run the cleaning cycle with that side facing down.

Laptops

The sweep can help with dusty laptop speakers. For spilled liquid, switch the laptop off and unplug it right away and seek repair, because sound will not clear a spill.

Clean a speaker without opening the phone

Sound cleaning works from the outside, so the device stays sealed. It helps with water in the grille and light dust, and it will not fix a damaged driver or water that has reached the circuit board.

Safety and limits

  • Keep the speaker away from your ears while it plays, and keep children and pets away.
  • Do not run it for long periods with the volume turned up on a damaged speaker.
  • Results vary. Sound can move small amounts of water, but it cannot dry a phone or reverse corrosion. If the phone was dropped in salt water, rinse it with clean water, dry it and get it checked.
